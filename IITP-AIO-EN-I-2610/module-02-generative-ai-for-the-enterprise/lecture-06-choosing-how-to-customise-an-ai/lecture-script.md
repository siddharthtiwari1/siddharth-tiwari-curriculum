## Topics

- What fine-tuning (further training a model on your own examples) changes
- Fine-tuning, RAG and prompting compared
- Cost of each option
- Knowledge gap (it lacks the facts: use RAG) or behaviour gap (it answers the wrong way: fine-tune)
- Hands-on: For three supplied assistant failures, decide whether prompting, RAG or fine-tuning fixes each, including the Uber refund-support case, then compare the cost of the three options in a sheet, from supplied unit costs, at 10,000, 100,000 and 10 million support queries a month for an airline such as IndiGo

## Learning objectives

- Explain what fine-tuning changes in a model that prompting cannot change
- Compare fine-tuning, RAG and prompt engineering as remedies for a stated model failure
- Compare the cost of prompting, RAG and fine-tuning for a stated use case
- Select between fine-tuning and RAG, justifying by whether the gap is knowledge or behaviour
