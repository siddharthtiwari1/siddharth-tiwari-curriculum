## Topics

- Approval rates by group: a simple fairness measure
- Two ways to measure fair (equal approval rates, or equal mistakes for people who qualify) and why you usually cannot have both when groups start from different rates (base rates)
- Choosing a definition by who is harmed
- Explainability: explaining a decision in plain language to the person affected
- Who is accountable when an automated decision causes harm
- Choosing a bias mitigation by where the bias entered
- Hands-on: Work out approval rates by group from supplied data, write a plain-language explanation of one rejection, then, if time allows, discuss as a class who was accountable in the 2024 Air Canada chatbot ruling

## Learning objectives

- Compute a fairness metric comparing model outcomes across two groups
- Explain why two fairness definitions cannot both hold when base rates differ
- Select a fairness definition for a stated decision, justifying by who is harmed by each kind of error
- Construct a plain-language explanation of an automated decision for the person affected
- Analyze who is accountable for a harm caused by an automated decision
- Recommend a bias mitigation, justifying by the stage at which the bias entered
